Conduct the orchestra

A string orchestra is tuning up, and it plays only on your beat.

  1. Raise your hand The tuning stops. They're waiting for you.
  2. Beat down Each beat plays the next. Your pace sets the tempo; bigger beats play louder.
  3. Close your hand Everyone stops together. Finish big and see what happens.

Point left for the cellos and basses, right for the violins. Raise your other hand to swell. Hold keys on the piano and choose Your chords to lead them through your own harmony.

How to play

Play

The middle row of your keyboard, A to the apostrophe, plays white keys from C. The row above, W E T Y U O P, plays the black keys.

The middle row of letters plays the white keys; the row above plays the black keys. You can also click, tap, or drag across the keys for a glissando. Tapping lower on a key plays it louder.

What the strings show

You're looking under the lid. Each key's hammer strikes its own strings: one thick copper string in the low bass, two in the tenor, three thin steel ones above.

Hold the pedal and play. The felt dampers lift, and strings whose overtones match your note start to ring on their own. That's sympathetic resonance. The number of loops on a string tells you which overtone it's answering.

The highest strings have no dampers at all, so they always ring on, just like on a real piano.

Play in a key

Choose a key to mark its scale on the keyboard and see each chord's role in it: I, IV, V7 and so on. Keys 1–7 play the key's chords; hold Shift for sevenths.

Turn on Stay in key and the middle letter row plays only notes of the scale, with the top row an octave higher.

Conduct

Choose Conduct and a string orchestra tunes up. With the camera, raise your hand to quiet them, then beat down in the air. Every beat plays the next beat of the music. Your pace is the tempo, the size of your beat is the volume, and pointing left or right brings the low strings or the violins forward. Raise your second hand to swell; close your hand to cut them off. No camera? Press and drag down on the stage with your mouse or finger, and press Esc to cut off.

Pick what they play at the top: a Canon after Pachelbel, a Lament, an Anthem, a Nocturne, or Your chords, which follows whatever you hold on the piano.

Record and share

Press Record, play, then Stop. Copy link creates a link that plays your take back for anyone who opens it. Nothing is uploaded: the whole take is written into the link.

With a MIDI keyboard, choose Sound, then Connect, to play with your own touch and pedal.
